01 The problem

Every company measures sentiment. None measure respect.

Engagement surveys ask how people feel. They miss the thing that actually drives attrition, silence, and burnout: whether people are listened to and treated with dignity in the room. “Toxic but polite” sails straight through a sentiment score.

What sentiment sees

“Thanks for the input! We’ll take it from here.”

Reads as positive. Cheerful words, high score.

vs.

What actually happened

A person was dismissed and stopped contributing.

A respect problem — invisible to the tools companies buy today.

02 The broken incumbent

Companies spend a fortune on surveys no one answers honestly.

Engagement surveys are expensive, run once or twice a year, and — worst of all — people don’t tell the truth on them. The fear of being identified and facing retaliation means candor never makes it to the form. Leadership pays a lot to learn very little.

The survey trap

  • Costly and annual — stale by the time results land.
  • Fear of retaliation kills candor; answers are cover-your-back, not truth.
  • Self-reported and gameable — a mirror that shows leaders nothing real.

What SugApp gives leadership

  • Continuous — from real behavior, not a once-a-year form.
  • Aggregated and k-anonymized — no one is ever exposed, so nothing is hidden out of fear.
  • A clear org-wide and team-wide picture of how each team is growing through self-reflection — the honest signal surveys were supposed to provide.
03 The moment

Built for the most uncertain workplace in a generation.

AI transformation is reorganizing every team at once — new tools, redefined roles, restructures, and real anxiety about who’s still needed. In that turbulence, how people treat each other is what decides whether change lands or your best people quietly leave. That’s exactly when leaders are flying blind.

Change breaks trust first

Reorgs and AI rollouts erode psychological safety long before they show up in output or in a quarterly survey.

Sentiment is noise now

In upheaval, mood swings with the daily news. Respect & listening is the durable signal of whether a team is intact.

The cost is silent

Disengagement and regretted attrition surface months late. Respect is the early-warning light that comes on first.

04 Why now

The signal was always there. The reader just arrived.

Work is text now

Slack, Teams, and chat hold the real record of how colleagues treat each other — dense, continuous, and unmined.

LLMs can finally read nuance

Judging acknowledgement, dismissal, or credit-sharing needs context, not keywords. That capability is new.

Trust is the gating factor

Post-privacy-backlash, the winner isn’t whoever reads the most — it’s whoever people trust to. That’s a design choice we make on line one.

05 What it is

A respect & listening layer for the modern workplace.

Connect a channel. SugApp reads the conversation with consent, and produces two things:

  • A private mirror for each person — their own words, what’s landing well, and one concrete thing to try next.
  • An anonymized Respect Index for the org — trends over time, never a name, never a quote.

The scoring engine is powered by a proprietary, validated behavioral taxonomy — our core IP, held as a trade secret and not shown here.

06 How culture actually shifts

Culture changes when people see themselves — not when they’re told.

Culture doesn’t shift with yearly surveys, performance reviews, or 1:1s — those are watched, rated, and remembered. It shifts with self-reflection, the part every program skips. The private mirror creates a quiet feedback loop no policy can: each person sees, in their own words, how they’re landing — and adjusts one small thing.

4 The room shifts

Because no one is watching but you, people actually use it — no fear, no scoring, no manager in the middle. One person changing one behavior, repeated across a team, is what slowly moves the needle — and the Respect Index turns that invisible shift into a trend leaders can finally see.

07 Self-managing teams

Teams that steady themselves through change — no surveillance required.

In uncertain times, teams need to self-correct fast. SugApp gives a team a shared, private signal of its own respect & listening health — a mirror for the group, not a report up the chain. Strain surfaces to the team itself, in time to do something about it.

A team mirror, not a manager’s dashboard

The team sees its own health first and owns the response — support, not scrutiny.

Early warning during change

Rising dismissiveness and falling acknowledgement show up weeks before they show up in resignations.

Self-correction

Shared awareness lets a team reset its own norms mid-reorg — the fastest, cheapest intervention there is.

Leaders get aggregate team health and trend — never individuals — so the whole org can navigate turbulence on the same instrument.

08 Why we win

Trust is the product, not the disclaimer.

Anyone can score text. The hard part — and the moat — is being the tool a workforce will actually allow in, especially when they’re already anxious about AI watching them. We engineered that in from the first commit:

Opt-in, with a real opt-out

No one is scored without consent. Withdrawing deletes what was collected. Enforced in code, not policy.

You see yourself first

The person always sees their own feedback before anyone else — it’s a mirror, not a report to your manager.

The org sees aggregates only

Team-level trends, k-anonymized; small teams are suppressed. Never a name, never a quote, up the chain.

Authenticated identity (SSO)

Sign-in-with-Slack ties consent to a verified identity — no impersonation, reliable opt-out.
